Hexacopter Wrench Research

Designed hexacopter drone and developed control systems through ROS 2 and Gazebo simulations for drones performing aerial manipulation as part of an Unmanned Aerial Vehicle (UAV) Project with Northrop Grumman in Wenlong Zhang’s Robotics and Intelligent SystEms (RISE) Lab.

Main Goal

Build testbed that records and filters Wrench (force/torque) data acting on a hexacopter using a robotic arm.

Robotic Arm and Force-Torque Sensor

To manipulate and measure the wrench data of the hexacopter drone, we used the Universal Robobtics UR5e robotic arm, coupled with the FT-300 force/torque sensor. The FT-300 measured force and torque acting on the endeffector along 3 axis, z, y, and z. I connected the UR5e to a router to allow for wireless control and monitoring.

  • FT-300

Hexacopter Drone

I designed a custom hexacopter drone, integrating off-the-shelf and custom made components, that would attach to the UR5e so that it's wrench forces could be measured. For the arms and base plate, I chose parts from DJI, spcifically their F450 series. I modeled the top plate, laser cutting it out of acrylic, and the UR5e mount, 3D printing it from PLA. Before construction, parts were tested individually and as a full assembly in a SolidWorks FEA analysis. Electronics wise, there were 6 motors, 6 ESCs, battery connector, a radio transmitter, transmitter, PX4 based control module, and USB XRCE bridge module. All hand wired and soldered together.
